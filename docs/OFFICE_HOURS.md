# Office Hours — StoneSignal

**Mode:** Startup diagnostic (pre-product)
**Position:** The broad “StockX for diamonds” pitch is too ambitious and liability-heavy. The credible wedge is a fast, transparent quote audit that prepares a buyer to verify a ring with independent sources.

## Six forcing questions

### 1. Demand reality
Current evidence is indirect: falling wholesale prices, large retail markups, and anxious buyer discussion. None proves willingness to pay. A $49 purchase test and refund behavior matter more than waitlist signups.

### 2. Painful status quo
A first-time buyer compares inconsistent jeweler pages, asks Reddit, decodes a grading report, and negotiates without an apples-to-apples total. The cost is hours of research plus fear of a $1,000–$4,000 overpayment or a non-returnable mistake.

### 3. Desperate specificity
The initial customer is an engagement-ring buyer within 14 days of purchase, holding a written quote for a lab-grown center stone and setting, who is anxious about markup and has no trusted jeweler in the family.

### 4. Narrowest paid wedge
For $49, deliver within 24 hours: a comparison band, certificate/report verification steps, markup flags, return-policy gaps, and five dealer questions. Do not claim gem authentication or appraisal.

### 5. Observation plan
Watch 8 buyers screen-share how they currently compare two quotes without guidance. Then watch 5 use the audit unassisted. Record where they cannot translate a report into a decision, which inputs they omit, and whether the questions change a dealer conversation.

### 6. Future-fit
As synthetic stones become cheaper and visually less differentiable, trusted transaction evidence becomes more important—but commodity price comparison becomes easier. The product must compound through verified outcomes and jeweler response data, not generic AI advice.

## Premise challenge

1. The job is not “authenticate a diamond online”; it is “reduce uncertainty before money changes hands.”
2. A deterministic audit with source provenance earns more trust than an opaque AI verdict.
3. Independent physical inspection remains outside the product; pretending otherwise would destroy trust.

## Approaches considered

### A — Quote checklist (minimal viable)
- **Effort:** S · **Risk:** Low
- Collect quote details and produce a comparison band plus verification checklist.
- Fast to validate, explainable, and appropriate for a static demo.
- Limited defensibility; benchmark upkeep is manual.

### B — Concierge verdict (recommended)
- **Effort:** M · **Risk:** Medium
- Human-in-the-loop review with structured evidence, report checks, dealer questions, and a 24-hour SLA.
- Tests willingness to pay while capturing proprietary decision data.
- Operationally intensive and must avoid appraisal claims.

### C — Verification network (ideal architecture)
- **Effort:** XL · **Risk:** High
- Independent jewelers inspect stones, escrow evidence, and issue portable StoneSignal records.
- Stronger trust and network effects; significant logistics, insurance, fraud, and regulatory burden.

## Assignment
Recruit eight buyers who already possess a written lab-grown ring quote and observe their unassisted comparison workflow before charging for anything.
